The Last Light of Saltmere
The storm hit the Silver Gull just after sunset. Waves crashed over the wooden
deck, the sails snapped in the wind and thunder rolled across a purple sky.
Captain Mara, a young pirate with a red bandana, held the helm while her little
pug Biscuit barked at every flash of lightning. Old Finn, the ship's cook, ran up
from the galley holding a wet scroll he had found inside a floating bottle. "Captain,
read this!" he shouted. The scroll was a torn map, signed by the keeper of the
Saltmere lighthouse: "The light has gone out. Without it, every ship on this coast
will sink. Find the Sun Crystal and bring it home." Mara rang the ship's brass bell
three times and turned the wheel towards the dark shore.
The Silver Gull reached a quiet beach under a sky full of stars. Broken barrels and
old nets lay on the white sand, and a cold wind blew between the palm trees. Next
to a small wooden hut, an old woman in a long dark coat was waiting with an unlit
lantern. She was Agnes, the lighthouse keeper. "A sea witch stole the Sun Crystal
and hid it under the waves," she said sadly. "Take this lantern. When you light it
with the fire of my hearth, it will show you the way through the deep." Mara
picked up the lantern and lit it in the campfire, and it began to glow with a soft
blue light. Biscuit ran along the shore, barking at the waves, and Agnes pointed at
the black water beyond the rocks: "That is where the sunken city sleeps."
Mara dived into the sea holding the blue lantern. Below the surface, a sunken city
opened in front of her: broken stone columns covered in coral, green seaweed
dancing in the current and schools of bright fish swimming between the ruins. In
the middle of a ruined temple stood Morwen, the sea witch, with a pointed hat and
glowing eyes. "Nobody leaves my kingdom without answering my riddle," she said
with a smile. "What has a face but no eyes, and hands but cannot hold?" Mara
thought for a moment and answered: "A clock." The witch laughed, and the coral
around the temple began to shine with magic. "Clever girl. The crystal is in the
cave of the goblin king, at the end of the coral path." On a stone altar, an ancient
inscription warned: "Only the brave may wake the sun."
The underwater path ended inside a dark cave full of stalactites and glowing
purple mushrooms. Gold coins and treasure chests were piled everywhere, and in
the deepest corner a small goblin king sat on a throne made of bones, guarding an
enormous locked chest. "Thieves! Thieves in my cave!" he screamed, and jumped
on his throne. Near the entrance, half hidden among the coins, Mara found a big
pink seashell that sang softly when the wind passed through it. She picked it up,
bowed politely and offered it to the goblin king. He held it to his ear, heard the
song of the sea and started to dance with joy. "Fine, fine! Take the key, but do not
touch my gold!" he said, and threw a rusty iron key at her feet. Mara picked up the
key and opened the big chest, and a warm golden light filled the whole cave.
Inside, the Sun Crystal was shining like a small sun.
At dawn, Mara climbed the long stone stairs of the Saltmere lighthouse on top of
the cliff, with Biscuit running ahead of her. At the top, Agnes the keeper waited
next to the giant empty lamp, surrounded by old books and maps of the coast. On
a small wooden table, inside an open velvet box, the Sun Crystal was waiting
where Mara had left it. She picked it up and placed it inside the lamp. For a
moment there was silence, and then a powerful golden beam burst out across the
ocean, chasing away the last storm clouds. Far below, the Silver Gull rang its bell,
and Old Finn waved his hat from the deck. "Saltmere will never be dark again,"
said Agnes, and she wrote Mara's name in the great book of the lighthouse. Biscuit
barked happily as the sun rose over the sea.
